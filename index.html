Oxide grows fast, then slows as oxygen must diffuse through it

The Deal–Grove model: oxidant reacts with silicon at the Si/SiO₂ interface. Thin oxides grow at a constant rate; thick ones grow as √t because the oxidant has to cross the oxide first. The oxide eats into the wafer as it grows.

How it works, how it is checked, and what it leaves out

The models

Oxide thickness x solves the Deal–Grove relation, with Arrhenius rate constants for dry O₂ or steam:

x² + A·x = B·(t + τ)   B = C₁e−E₁/kT, B/A = C₂e−E₂/kT

For (100) silicon, C₂ is divided by 1.68. Silicon consumed is 0.44·x. The wafer colour comes from reflectance of air / SiO₂ / Si, integrated against the CIE colour-matching functions.

An implant is a Gaussian set by projected range Rp and straggle ΔRp. The anneal solves Fick's second law with a θ-scheme on a uniform grid:

∂C/∂t = D ∂²C/∂x²   (I − θrA)Cⁿ⁺¹ = (I + (1−θ)rA)Cⁿ

θ = 0 is explicit, ½ is Crank–Nicolson, 1 is fully implicit. Each step is a tridiagonal solve. The explicit scheme is only stable for r ≤ ½.

How it is checked

  • The finite-difference profile is compared with the exact solution at every frame: a Gaussian with σ² = ΔRp² + 2Dt plus its mirror image at the surface, or Cs·erfc(x/2√(Dt)) for predeposition. Current max error: – of the peak.
  • Junction depth, solver vs exact: –.
  • Dopant dose is conserved by the zero-flux surface. Current dose error: –.
  • Halving Δt cuts the Crank–Nicolson error about 4× and the implicit error about 2×, confirming second- and first-order accuracy in time.
  • The computed oxide colours line up with the standard colour chart: about 50 nm tan, 120 nm royal blue, 150 nm light blue, 200 nm gold, 300 nm blue.

What it leaves out

  • Dry oxides under about 25 nm grow faster than Deal–Grove predicts (the Massoud regime); the checkbox is the classic workaround.
  • Pressure, chlorine and doping effects on oxidation rate.
  • Oxidation and diffusion are separate here: no dopant segregation at the moving interface, and no oxidation-enhanced diffusion.
  • Diffusivities are intrinsic. Heavy doping and implant damage make real profiles diffuse faster (concentration-dependent and transient-enhanced diffusion).
  • Implants are symmetric Gaussians with no channeling; real profiles are skewed (Pearson IV) and best taken from SRIM or Monte Carlo.
  • One dimension only, so no lateral spread under a mask edge yet.
